## The problem

{/* TO DO: what wasn't working, for whom, and how you knew. */}

Workday Learning and Sana Learn were two separate products. The aim was to bring Workday's learning platform together with Sana's AI-powered experience under one vision: Workday Learning, powered by Sana.

## What I did

### Leading the integration

I led design for the Workday Learning and Sana Learn integration. I led the UX team through the integration design work and set the design leadership goals, working closely with product, engineering and solution architecture so the design carried through to launch.

The vision rested on three ideas:

- **AI-native and personalised.**
- **Enterprise-grade scale and governance.**
- **One seamless experience.**

### Setting AI design strategy

I define and drive AI design strategy across our product portfolio, so UX has a clear, principled point of view in a fast-moving space.

### Giving design an early voice

I influence product direction at VP level, so design is part of roadmap and planning conversations from the start. With product and engineering, I plan and prioritise the roadmap, balancing user needs, business goals and team capacity.

### Building the team and its craft

- **Coaching:** regular 1:1s, structured feedback, and advocating for designers' progression.
- **Critique:** weekly design critiques to raise the quality bar and keep craft standards high.
- **Hiring:** leading UX design hiring end to end, from defining the role to the final decision, and contributing to hiring in product and engineering.
- **Culture:** a collaborative, psychologically safe team where designers can take creative risks and do their best work.
- **Community:** organising UX community events at Workday's Dublin office.

## Outcome

The design carried through to launch, bringing Workday's learning platform and Sana's AI-powered experience together as one product: Workday Learning, powered by Sana.
